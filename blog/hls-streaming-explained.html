What is HLS Streaming? A Beginner's Guide to M3U8

Published: May 2026  |  Reading time: 5 min

What is HLS?

HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) is a video streaming protocol developed by Apple. It's the most widely used method for delivering video over the internet — YouTube, Netflix, TikTok, and virtually every major video platform uses some variant of HLS.

Instead of sending one giant video file, HLS chops the video into thousands of tiny segments (usually 2–10 seconds each) and sends them one at a time. This makes streaming faster, more reliable, and adaptive to your internet speed.

How HLS Works: The Key Parts

The M3U8 Playlist (The Index)

An M3U8 file is a text file — a playlist that lists every video segment in order. Think of it as a table of contents for a video. Your browser reads the M3U8 file first, then fetches each TS segment one by one.

TS Segments (The Video Chunks)

TS (MPEG Transport Stream) files are the actual video chunks. Each .ts file contains a few seconds of video + audio. A 10-minute video might be split into 200+ TS segments.

Adaptive Bitrate (Auto Quality)

HLS can serve multiple quality versions of the same video. A master playlist lists available quality levels (360p, 720p, 1080p), each with its own sub-playlist. Your player switches between qualities automatically based on your connection speed — that's why YouTube quality sometimes drops when your internet is slow.

Why Does Almost Every Site Use HLS?

  • Works everywhere — Any HTTP server can serve HLS. No special streaming server needed
  • CDN friendly — TS segments can be cached by standard CDNs (Cloudflare, Akamai, etc.)
  • Adaptive quality — Viewers always get the best possible quality for their connection
  • Firewall safe — Uses standard HTTP port 80/443, never blocked by firewalls
  • DRM support — HLS supports encryption, so premium platforms can protect their content
